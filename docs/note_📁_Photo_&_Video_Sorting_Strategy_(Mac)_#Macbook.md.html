📁 Photo & Video Sorting Strategy (Mac) #Macbook



Step 1: Set Up a Master Folder Structure

Create a central folder (e.g. All Photos & Videos) somewhere easy to access (like your Desktop or Pictures folder), and inside it create these main subfolders:
• Family
• Friends
• Days Out
• Pets
• Events (birthdays, weddings, holidays, etc.)
• Screenshots (you’ll probably find lots of these)
• Random or Misc
• To Review (temporary home for unclear ones)

You can refine or add more later, but keep it simple to start.

⸻

Step 2: Gather All Files in One Place
1. Copy, not move, all folders from old backups into a new “Working” folder (e.g. Photo Sort Working Folder).
2. Do this in batches if you’re low on disk space.
3. Don’t worry if files are already duplicated — we’ll fix that.

⸻

Step 3: Use a Duplicate Finder (Optional but Helpful)

Install a free or cheap duplicate file finder. Good Mac options:
• Gemini 2 (has a good free trial)
• Duplicate File Finder Remover (on Mac App Store, free with optional Pro upgrade)

These tools will help you spot and remove exact duplicates safely.

⸻

Step 4: Auto-Sort by File Type & Date
1. Use Finder’s “Group by Kind” or “Group by Date” feature to start separating photos from videos.
2. Consider creating two folders within your working folder:
• Photos - To Sort
• Videos - To Sort

Then move files accordingly.

⸻

Step 5: Use AI or Metadata to Speed Things Up

Your Mac can help sort:
• Preview photos in large icon view
• Use Quick Look (press spacebar) to rapidly scan images.
• Use apps like:
• Photos app – import files and let it group people, locations, and dates automatically.
• Pixave or Eagle (for creative file organizing, small cost).
• Apple Photos (iCloud Photos) – if you enable it, it’ll auto-categorize people, scenes, and memories.

⸻

Step 6: Manual Sorting by Theme

As you go through images:
• Move them manually into your folders: Family, Pets, etc.
• Use multi-select (Cmd + Click or drag) to move similar groups.

To speed this up:
• Start with recent years, or work backwards.
• Do it in short 30-minute sessions to avoid burnout.

⸻

Step 7: Add Tags (Optional but Powerful)

Right-click a file or group > Tags:
• Red = Needs Editing
• Blue = Already Sorted
• Green = Favourite

You can later search/filter by tag in Finder.

⸻

Step 8: Back Up Your Sorted Library

Once sorted:
• Back up to an external drive.
• Consider syncing to iCloud Photos, Google Photos or OneDrive so it’s protected and searchable.

⸻

💡 Bonus Tips
• Want AI to help detect photos of people/pets? Photos app (or Google Photos) is excellent at auto-recognizing faces.
• Want to rename files by date? Try A Better Finder Rename or NameChanger (free).
• For video thumbnails, use IINA player or VLC to preview quickly.

✅ Step-by-Step Guide: Organising Photos & Videos (Mac + Home Cloud)

⸻

STEP 1: Set Up Your Folder Structure (on Home Cloud)

Create a main folder on your home cloud like:

Photos and Videos (Master)
│
├── Photos
│ ├── Family
│ ├── Friends
│ ├── Days Out
│ ├── Pets
│ ├── Events
│ ├── Misc
│ └── Screenshots
│
└── Videos
├── Family
├── Friends
├── Days Out
├── Pets
├── Events
├── Misc
└── Screen Recordings

💡 Tip: You can add year folders later if it helps (e.g. Family > 2021, Pets > 2020).

⸻

STEP 2: Create a “Working” Folder for Sorting

On your Mac (but linked to the home cloud folder), create:
• Sorting Area
• Photos to Sort
• Videos to Sort

Start copying batches of photos/videos from your backup folders into these two.

⸻

STEP 3: Find & Remove Duplicates

Download a Mac app to clean up identical files:
• Free Option: [Duplicate File Finder Remover](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/duplicate-file-finder-remover/id1069172483)
• Better Option: [Gemini 2](https://macpaw.com/gemini) (free trial then £)

📌 Do this before serious sorting so you don’t waste time organizing duplicates.

⸻

STEP 4: Preview & Tag Quickly

In Finder:
1. Use Gallery View or Quick Look (spacebar) to preview.
2. Use colour Tags for bulk labelling:
• Red = Needs Sorting
• Blue = Sorted
• Green = Favourite

This helps if you only do short sorting sessions.

⸻

STEP 5: Batch Move to Categories

Drag and drop into your category folders (Family, Pets, etc.). Do this based on:
• Who’s in the photo
• What’s happening
• Background clues (school, beach, park, home)
• File date (you can “Sort by Date Created”)

💡 You can sort by kind (photo/video) using Finder > View > Group By > Kind.

⸻

STEP 6: Use Apple Photos (Optional but Powerful for Face Detection)

Even though you don’t want to store the full library in iCloud, you can use Apple Photos for tagging/face sorting:

How:
1. Open Photos app on Mac.
2. Create a temporary Photos Library and import a batch to let it scan faces, people, pets.
3. Use its “People” or “Memories” feature to quickly identify who’s in what.
4. Export those sorted images back into your cloud folder.

Then delete them from Photos to keep storage clean.

⸻

STEP 7: Rename for Easy Searching (Optional)

If you want files renamed to include dates or subjects:
• Use [NameChanger (Free)](https://mrrsoftware.com/namechanger/)
• Or [A Better Finder Rename](https://www.publicspace.net/ABetterFinderRename/)

Examples:
• 2022-07-10_Bella_Birthday.jpg
• 2023-Raffy-AdventurePark.mov

⸻

STEP 8: Sync Highlights to iCloud (Optional)

If you want a curated album available on your iPhone:
1. Create an “iCloud Highlights” folder.
2. Copy selected favourites there.
3. Sync only that folder to iCloud Photos.

✅ Keeps your full library off iCloud but lets you enjoy/share your best stuff easily.

⸻

🧠 Bonus Tips
• Do this in 25-minute sorting sessions to avoid burnout.
• Start with the most recent or the most important folders (like family or kids).
• Regularly back up your sorted folders to Time Machine or another external drive.

🧠 PART 2: Help with Finding People & Pets in Photos

🧰 Using Apple Photos on Mac:
1. Open the Photos app
2. Import a batch from your “Photos to Sort” folder
3. Let it process — it will auto-detect:
• Faces (Bella, Raffy, yourself, etc.)
• Pets
• Scenes (parks, beaches, indoors, etc.)
4. Click “People” in the left sidebar and assign names
5. Use “Albums > Smart Albums” to create folders like:
• All photos of Raffy
• All photos with more than 1 person
• All photos with a pet

Then export the sorted files back to your home cloud folder where they belong.
